Music: Language We All Speak
Music is a universal ability shared by almost all humans. Unlike other skills, we do not need formal training to enjoy or create it. However, the reason why we have music is a mystery. In contrast, the purpose of language is obvious. Language allows us to communicate complex ideas and organize society. Because of this, some scientists, like Steven Pinker, previously thought music was not essential. He believed it was just for fun and had no use for human intelligence.

Recently, this view has changed. Over the last decade, new publications in neuroscience have shown that music and language are intricately connected. Traditionally, experts emphasized the mathematical side of music. For example, Pythagoras discovered that the physical dimension of a string affects the sound it makes. This led to the theory that music is abstract and unrelated to the real world.

Researchers like David Schwartz disagree. They argue that our preference for certain musical sounds comes from speech. Schwartz analyzed thousands of speech sounds. His research revealed that the frequency patterns in speech are very similar to musical scales. This suggests that music mimics the human voice. Basically, we like music because it sounds like us. This raises an interesting question: did music come first, or did language? While we cannot know for sure, this demonstrates that both come from the same biological system.

This research also affects how we view animals. Previous studies indicated that animals lack the perception to appreciate human music. However, Schwartz argues that we are testing them with the wrong sounds. If animals have a musical sense, it would likely be based on their own natural sounds. Therefore, a cat might enjoy a tune made of meows, even if it dislikes Mozart.

Finally, the connection is clear with babies. Research shows a continuum between speech and song when mothers talk to babies. Mothers use a melodic voice to regulate the infant's emotional state. This happens in every culture. This evidence suggests that music is a necessity for human bonding and development, rather than just a luxury.
